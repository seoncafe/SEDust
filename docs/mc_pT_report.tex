\begin{abstract}
We implement the Monte Carlo (MC) algorithm of
\citet{DraineAnderson1985} for tracking the temperature of a single
interstellar dust grain through individual photon-absorption events,
extend it with a selectable heat-capacity model, and reproduce
Figures~24.5 and 24.6 of \citet{DrainePIIM2011}.  The standalone
implementation is kept for visualization; a subroutine-form API is
then used to integrate $P(T)$ over a grain size distribution and
build an emergent SED.  The SED builder exposes a runtime flag
\texttt{use\_mc\_for\_large\_grain}.  In the default mode
($\texttt{=\,F}$), small (stochastic) grains use the Monte Carlo
engine while large (equilibrium) grains use the analytic
\texttt{calc\_Teq} solver -- the same hybrid logic the production
solver uses, but with MC replacing the
\citet{GuhathakurtaDraine1989} matrix solver in the stochastic
branch.  The implementation divides the radiation field into discrete
photon absorptions and continuous heating at an adaptive wavelength
cutoff $\lambda_c(a)=hc/[\delta T_{\rm thresh}\Teq\Ctot(\Teq)]$, limits
each cooling step to a 5\% temperature change, integrates every sampled
waiting interval completely, and stops with an explicit status if a
step or sample budget is exhausted. Each run first relaxes the grain
from its radiative-equilibrium temperature for a fixed preparation time
that is excluded from all recorded quantities. Neutral and ionized PAHs
are calculated separately, and each grain spectrum is normalized to its
absorbed power.  For the HD23 astrodust+PAH model in the Mathis field
at $\log U=0.20$, the default-mode MC SED agrees with the production
solver to within $0.2\%$ in the band-integrated 5--30, 30--300, and
300--\SI{3000}{\micro\meter} power (ratios $0.998$, $1.001$, $1.001$)
and is $3.9\%$ low in 1--\SI{5}{\micro\meter} (ratio $0.961$). The
all-MC mode gives the same band ratios to $0.0003$, and the
radiation-equivalent MC temperature of every equilibrium-gate grain
lies within $-0.13\%$ and $+0.01\%$ of $\Teq$ for
$a=0.032$--\SI{5}{\micro\meter}.  Before the spectral normalization the
recorded trajectories close their energy budget to between $-1.08\%$
and $+0.08\%$ of the absorbed energy, grain by grain. The
photon-wavelength sampler follows the supported absorption spectrum:
five graphite-grain tests of $2\times10^6$ draws each found no photons
below the $0.0912\umc$ Lyman edge, and the measured mean energies
agree with the exact means of the implemented distributions within
0.60 standard errors (Section~\ref{sec:photon_sampling}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A dust grain in the interstellar medium absorbs starlight photons
discretely.  When the grain is macroscopic ($a \gtrsim 0.03\umc$),
many absorption events occur per radiative cooling time and the grain
sits at a well-defined equilibrium temperature $\Teq(a)$ at which the
mean absorbed power balances the radiative cooling.  Smaller grains,
however, do not have enough heat capacity to absorb the energy of a
single starlight photon without making a large, transient excursion
in temperature.  Each photon absorption raises the grain's
temperature by tens to hundreds of Kelvin; between events the grain
cools radiatively back toward $\Teq$.  The temperature history $T(t)$
is a sequence of sharp upward spikes followed by long, slow
cooling tails, as illustrated in Figure~24.5 of \citet{DrainePIIM2011}.

Because the emission spectrum scales steeply with temperature
($\propto T^4$ in total luminosity, with strong wavelength
dependence through the Planck function and the cross section),
the spectral energy distribution (SED) of small grains is dominated
by the high-temperature spikes, not by $\Teq$.  Quantitative treatment
requires the probability distribution $P(T)$, which is the fraction
of time the grain spends at each temperature in steady state.

There are two standard ways to obtain $P(T)$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Monte Carlo} \citep{DraineAnderson1985}.  Simulate the
  stochastic sequence of absorption events directly and accumulate a
  time-weighted histogram of $T(t)$.
\item \textbf{Discretized steady-state matrix solve}
  \citep{GuhathakurtaDraine1989, DraineLi2001}.  Discretize $T$ into
  bins, build a transition matrix from absorption (upward) and
  Planck-averaged emission (downward) rates, and solve for the steady
  state.
\end{enumerate}
The matrix method is more efficient and is what the production
astrodust solver uses (\texttt{p\_sub.f90::calc\_P}).  The MC method
is conceptually simpler, has fewer numerical knobs, and provides a
useful independent cross-check.  This document records the MC
implementation kept under \verb|SEDust/mc/| and its validation.

\section{Algorithm}\label{sec:algo}

\subsection{Two-regime treatment of the absorbed radiation field}

Consider a spherical grain of radius $a$ in an isotropic radiation
field of mean intensity $\Jlam(\lambda)$.  The radiative energy
density per unit wavelength is
\begin{equation}
\ulam = \frac{4\pi}{c}\Jlam \quad
[\text{erg}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\,\umc^{-1}].
\end{equation}
The grain absorbs photons of wavelength $\lambda$ at a rate per unit
wavelength
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dRate}
\frac{dN_{\rm abs}}{dt\,d\lambda}
   = \pi a^2 \, \Qabs(a,\lambda)\,\frac{c\,\ulam\,\lambda}{hc}
   = \frac{\pi a^2}{h}\,\Qabs(a,\lambda)\,\ulam\,\lambda,
\end{equation}
where the photon energy is $E = hc/\lambda$ and we have used
$\ulam/E = (\ulam\,\lambda)/(hc)$ as the photon number density per
unit wavelength, multiplied by $c$ to get a photon flux density and
by $\pi a^2\,\Qabs$ to get the absorption rate.

\citet{DraineAnderson1985} split this rate into a continuous part
(long wavelengths, where photons arrive so frequently that the grain
heat content increases smoothly) and a stochastic part (short
wavelengths, where individual events are resolved).  Pick a cutoff
$\lambda_c$: the standalone driver \verb|main_mc.x| uses the namelist
value \verb|lam_c_um| (default $1000\umc$), while the SED builder and
the single-grain comparison driver use the adaptive cutoff of
Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_lamc}.  The continuous-heating rate per
unit surface area is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hcont}
H(a) \;\equiv\; \frac{c}{4}\!\int_{\lambda_c}^{\infty}\!
       \Qabs(a,\lambda)\,\ulam\,d\lambda
       \quad [{\rm erg}\,{\rm cm}^{-2}\,{\rm s}^{-1}].
\end{equation}
The stochastic event rate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rateEvt}
\Gamma_{\rm evt}(a) \;\equiv\;
       \int_{0}^{\lambda_c}\!
       \frac{dN_{\rm abs}}{dt\,d\lambda}\,d\lambda
       \quad [{\rm s}^{-1}].
\end{equation}
The division depends on $\lambda_c$ and the radiation field, and both
terms are retained in the calculation. For the smallest astrodust
grains in the Mathis field at $U=1.585$, the adaptive cutoff reaches
the long-wavelength grid limit ($3.936\times10^4\umc$), so $H(a)$
contains only the CMB absorption longward of that wavelength and is
of order $10^{-13}$ of the event power (Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_lamc}).

The tabulated wavelength grid generally has one interval straddling
$\lambda_c$.  That interval is split at $\lambda_c$ by linear
interpolation of the integrand: its blueward part is added to
$\Gamma_{\rm evt}$ (and to the photon-sampling CDF) and its redward
part to $H(a)$, so the cutoff bin is dropped from neither term.  The
sampling CDF is built from the same split integral as
$\Gamma_{\rm evt}$ and has its last node pinned at $\lambda_c$, so a
sampled photon always satisfies $\lambda \le \lambda_c$.  A grain with
no absorption blueward of $\lambda_c$ has $\Gamma_{\rm evt}=0$ and
belongs on the equilibrium path; \texttt{grain\_setup\_from\_cabs}
stops with a diagnostic rather than divide by a zero event rate.

\subsection{Cooling ODE between events}

Let $E_{\rm grain}(T)$ be the total grain enthalpy and
$\Ctot(T) \equiv dE_{\rm grain}/dT$ its total heat capacity.  Between
absorption events, the grain's energy evolves according to
\begin{equation}
\frac{dE_{\rm grain}}{dt} = P_{\rm heat} - P_{\rm emit}
   = 4\pi a^2 \bigl[\,H(a) - \QplT \sigmaSB T^4\,\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\QplT$ is the Planck-averaged absorption efficiency,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qpl}
\QplT \;\equiv\;
   \frac{\int \Qabs(a,\lambda)\,\Blam(T)\,d\lambda}
        {\int \Blam(T)\,d\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Dividing through by $\Ctot$ gives the cooling ODE used in the MC:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cooling}
\boxed{\;\frac{dT}{dt}
   = \frac{4\pi a^2}{\Ctot(T)}\,
     \bigl[\,H(a) - \QplT\,\sigmaSB\,T^4\,\bigr].\;}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:cooling}) is identical to Eq.~(2.9) of
\citet{DraineAnderson1985}, which writes it in the equivalent form
$dT/dt = (3/(a\,\Cvol(T)))\,(H - \QplT\sigmaSB T^4)$ using the heat
capacity per unit volume $\Cvol = \Ctot/(4\pi a^3/3)$.  We use the
total form because the enthalpy routines
(\texttt{sed/src/enthalpy\_v2.f90::enthalpy\_DL01} and the astrodust
enthalpy module) return the total grain enthalpy in erg.  The factor
$4\pi a^2$ in our form is the grain \emph{surface} area (heating and
emission are surface effects), and the $1/\Ctot$ absorbs the grain
volume implicit in the total heat capacity. The engine tabulates
$\QplT$ on a 2000-point logarithmic grid from 1 to $10^4$~K and uses
$\langle\Qabs\rangle_{1\,\rm K}$ below 1~K.

\subsection{The Monte Carlo loop}

Starting from $(t_0, T_0)$, one step of the MC simulation does the
following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item\textbf{Sample the inter-arrival time.}
  The event-arrival process is Poissonian with rate
  $\Gamma_{\rm evt}$, so the waiting time
  $\Delta t = -\ln U_1 / \Gamma_{\rm evt}$
  where $U_1 \in (0,1]$ is a uniform random number.
\item\textbf{Integrate Eq.~(\ref{eq:cooling}) over $[t_0, t_0+\Delta t]$.}
  The grain cools (or, in the rare regime where $H > \QplT\sigmaSB T^4$,
  heats) according to the continuous part of the radiation field.
  During this step we accumulate the time-weighted contribution to the
  $P(T)$ histogram and to the integrated emitted energy.
\item\textbf{Sample the absorbed photon.}
  Choose a wavelength $\lambda$ from the normalized PDF
  $p(\lambda) \propto dN_{\rm abs}/(dt\,d\lambda)$ on
  the supported interval below $\lambda_c$. The cumulative
  probability is stored on the physical wavelength knots. A binary
  search selects a positive-probability interval, and the integral
  of its linear photon density is inverted within that interval.
\item\textbf{Apply the enthalpy jump.}
  The absorbed photon energy $E_{\rm phot} = hc/\lambda$ goes into
  internal vibrational energy.  Solve
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:jump}
  E_{\rm grain}(T_{\rm after}) = E_{\rm grain}(T_{\rm before})
                                + E_{\rm phot}
  \end{equation}
  for $T_{\rm after}$. The enthalpy table locates a bracket; a
  safeguarded Newton--bisection inversion then evaluates the analytic
  $E_{\rm grain}(T)$ within that bracket. When adjacent representable
  temperatures bracket the target energy, the closer endpoint is
  accepted within a bounded enthalpy error.
\end{enumerate}

The loop is repeated for a prescribed number $\evt$ of recorded
events, or until a prescribed recorded time $t_{\rm max}$ is reached.
The calculations in this report use $\evt=5\times10^4$ (SED default),
$2\times10^5$ and $5\times10^5$ events per grain.

\subsection{Integrator for the cooling ODE}

For a $100\,\textrm{\AA}$ graphite grain in the $U=1$ Mathis field the
measured event rate is $\Gamma_{\rm evt}=2.06\times10^{-3}\,{\rm s}^{-1}$,
a mean inter-arrival time of $486$~s, which is long compared with the
radiative cooling after a photon spike.  The cooling
ODE~(\ref{eq:cooling}) is therefore mildly stiff: rapid initial decay
followed by a long, near-equilibrium tail.  Explicit Euler is
prohibitively slow because the step size near equilibrium is set by
the restoring rate, which is large.

The right-hand side of Eq.~(\ref{eq:cooling}) is
dominated, after the photon spike, by the $T^4$ Stefan--Boltzmann
emission term: with $H(a)\ll \QplT \sigmaSB T^4$ the equation reduces
to $dT/dt \simeq -(4\pi a^2/\Ctot)\QplT\sigmaSB T^4$.  Both $\Ctot(T)$
and $\QplT$ depend on $T$, so the trajectory is
not a single exponential -- it falls steeply at high $T$ and slowly
near $\Teq$.  Any closed-form fit (exponential, power-law $T^{-3}$
from constant-$C$ Stefan--Boltzmann) is an approximation; we therefore
integrate Eq.~(\ref{eq:cooling}) numerically and, when a continuous
$T(t)$ is needed for a figure, dump the actual integrator sub-step
samples rather than reconstructing from event boundaries.

We use a linearly-implicit (exponential) step.  At the current
temperature $T_0$, we form a one-sided finite difference to estimate
the local slope
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(T_0) \;\equiv\; \frac{d(dT/dt)}{dT}\bigg|_{T_0}
   \approx \frac{(dT/dt)_{1.01\,T_0} - (dT/dt)_{T_0}}{0.01\,T_0}.
\end{equation}
When $\Lambda < 0$ the dynamics is locally a stable relaxation
toward a local equilibrium temperature
$T_{\rm eq, loc} = T_0 - (dT/dt)_{T_0}/\Lambda$, and the linearized
solution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expstep}
T(t_0 + dt) \;=\; T_{\rm eq, loc} +
   \bigl(T_0 - T_{\rm eq, loc}\bigr)\,e^{\Lambda \, dt}
\end{equation}
is exact for the linearized equation.  We step by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stepcap}
dt = \min\!\Bigl(dt_{\rm left},\; \tfrac{\ln 2}{|\Lambda|},\;
     f_{\rm cap}\,\tfrac{T_0}{|dT/dt|_{T_0}}\Bigr),
     \qquad f_{\rm cap} = 0.05;
\end{equation}
once $|T_0 - T_{\rm eq, loc}|/T_0 < 10^{-3}$ we take the full
remaining $dt_{\rm left}$ in a single step at $T_{\rm eq,loc}$.
The cap $\ln 2 / |\Lambda|$ halves the distance to local equilibrium
per step.  The cap on the \emph{fractional} temperature change,
$f_{\rm cap}=5\%$ per step, bounds the linearization error: the
linearized solution~(\ref{eq:expstep}) is exact only for a locally
\emph{linear} ODE, whereas the true cooling is strongly super-linear
($dT/dt \propto -\QplT T^4$), so over a long step the linearization
lags the faster early cooling and the grain lingers at high $T$.
With the cap in place the recorded trajectories still emit slightly
more than they absorb (Section~\ref{sec:energy}); this residual does
not change between $5\times10^4$ and $5\times10^5$ events and is a
property of the step, not of the sampling.
The engines continue cooling through each sampled waiting interval,
in chunks of internal steps (300 for the adaptive engines, 4000 for
the fixed-grid engine). A budget of $10^6$ steps applies to each
waiting interval by default; exceeding it returns a failure status
and the unintegrated time rather than a truncated trajectory.

The $P(T)$ histogram and the emitted-energy bookkeeping sub-sample each
step at $N_{\rm sub}=8$ interior points of the sub-step trajectory,
each carrying weight $dt/N_{\rm sub}$, so a step that spans several
histogram bins deposits its time weight across all of them.
For $\Lambda \ge 0$ the step is a forward Euler step, also subject to
the $f_{\rm cap}$ limit.

The engine imposes a numerical temperature floor of $0.5$~K. A grain
at the floor whose cooling rate still points downward stays there for
the rest of its waiting interval, and that residence is reported
separately (\texttt{floor\_time\_out}). The floor is a lower limit of
the numerical representation, not a physical temperature: below 1~K
the cooling uses $\langle\Qabs\rangle_{1\,\rm K}$, and the formal
balance temperatures of the smallest grains lie far below 0.5~K
(Section~\ref{sec:adaptive_lamc}).

\section{Implementation}\label{sec:impl}

\subsection{Module layout}

The implementation lives under \verb|SEDust/mc/| and is split
into small modules that each do one thing.  Throughout, cgs units
are used (erg, cm, s) except that wavelength is in $\umc$ to match
the rest of the astrodust code.

\begin{itemize}
\item \verb|mc_heatcap.f90|: enthalpy $E_{\rm grain}(T,a)$ and heat
  capacity $\Ctot(T)=dE_{\rm grain}/dT$ for selectable compositions
  (Table~\ref{tab:hc}).  $\Ctot$ is computed by central differences on
  $E_{\rm grain}$, which works for all heat-capacity models without
  composition-specific analytic derivatives.

\item \verb|mc_rng.f90|: thread-local pseudo-random number generator
  (\textsf{xorshift64*}, period $2^{64}-1$) wrapped in a derived type
  \verb|rng_t|.  Each OpenMP thread owns its own state, so no locking
  is needed inside the MC inner loop.

\item \verb|mc_grain_type.f90|: derived type \verb|mc_grain_t| holding
  all single-grain pre-tabulated state ($\lambda$ grid, $\Qabs$, $\ulam$,
  $H_{\rm cont}$, $\Gamma_{\rm evt}$, photon-wavelength CDF, $T$ grid,
  $E_{\rm grain}(T)$, $\QplT$).  Instances are local to a thread.

\item \verb|mc_engine.f90|: the engine.  Two setup entry points:
  \verb|grain_setup_graphite| (D16 graphite sphere Q on a logarithmic
  grid from $10^{-3}$ to $10^4\umc$, used by the standalone validation
  path) and \verb|grain_setup_from_cabs| (arbitrary $\Cabs(\lambda)$
  on a caller-supplied grid, used by the SED builder and the
  single-grain comparison driver).  Three MC drivers with the same
  signature shape, differing only in the histogram strategy:
  \verb|mc_run_engine| (fixed logarithmic grid on $[0.5, 5000]$~K),
  \verb|mc_run_engine_2pass|, and
  \verb|mc_run_engine_buffered|.  All three return the time-weighted
  $P(T)$ histogram (as bin edges $T_{\rm edge}$, $dP/dT$, and
  $dP/d\ln T$) and the integrated absorbed and emitted energies; the
  adaptive engines additionally return the selected grid type
  (\texttt{is\_log\_out}) and the observed $T_{\rm min}/T_{\rm max}$.
  Each driver reports completion status, omitted waiting time, residence
  at the $0.5$~K numerical floor, time outside the histogram range,
  its number of temperature samples, and the change of stored grain
  enthalpy. A sample outside the histogram range or an exhausted sample
  capacity is an error, not a truncated $P(T)$. See
  Section~\ref{sec:adaptive} for the histogram algorithms. The engine
  also provides \verb|grain_radiative_equilibrium|, which balances
  radiation absorbed from the supplied field against thermal emission,
  and \verb|mc_equilibrate|, which advances the temperature and random
  state before any histogram or energy accounting begins.

\item \verb|mc_sed.f90|: SED builder.  Wraps an OpenMP parallel
  loop over the size distribution; each iteration creates its own
  \verb|mc_grain_t| and \verb|rng_t|, runs MC, and accumulates the
  thread-local contribution to $\lambda I_\lambda$.

\item \verb|main_mc.f90|: standalone single-grain driver
  (\verb|main_mc.x|).  Reads a namelist, runs one MC, writes
  \verb|<prefix>_PT.dat| and, with \verb|record_trajectory=.true.|,
  the trajectory files \verb|<prefix>_Tt.dat| and \verb|<prefix>_evt.dat|.

\item \verb|main_mc_sed.f90|: size-distribution SED driver
  (\verb|main_mc_sed.x|).  Reads a namelist, calls \verb|sed_init|
  from the astrodust code to load the grain Q table, size
  distribution, and PAH cross sections, then loops via \verb|mc_sed|.
  It calls \verb|sed_init| without the optional \verb|lam_min|, so the
  wavelength grid is the plain T-matrix $Q$-table grid
  (\SIrange{0.0912}{3.98e4}{\micro\meter}) and the extreme-ultraviolet
  extension available to the library does not enter here.

\item \verb|main_pT_compare.f90|: single-grain comparison driver
  (\verb|main_pT_compare.x|), Section~\ref{sec:per_grain_pT}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stationary recording and transient experiments}

Each MC driver first obtains a radiative-equilibrium temperature
from its configured grain and radiation field. The preparation trajectory
starts at this temperature and continues for a predetermined time
\begin{equation}
T_w = \max\!\left(10\tau_{\rm relax},\frac{100}{\Gamma_{\rm evt}}\right)
\end{equation}
when the discrete event rate $\Gamma_{\rm evt}$ is positive. For zero
event rate, $T_w=10\tau_{\rm relax}$. The second term gives 100
\emph{expected} absorption events, not a minimum actual count; in the
graphite runs of Section~\ref{sec:valid} the preparation absorbed
84--106 photons. The relaxation time is the larger of the equilibrium
enthalpy divided by absorbed power and the local thermal cooling time
when absorbed power is positive; only the local cooling time is used
when absorbed power vanishes. This duration does not by itself prove
statistical convergence of $P(T)$.
The discrete absorption rate is independent of temperature, so its
events form a homogeneous Poisson process. The recording starts at
$T_w$, which need not coincide with an event; the engine draws a fresh
exponential residual waiting time, consistent with Poisson
memorylessness.

The temperature and random state at the end of preparation start the
recorded trajectory. Its event count and $t_{\rm max}$ are recording
budgets. The preparation interval contributes neither histogram
residence nor elapsed time, event absorption, emission, or stored-energy
change to the reported run. The comparison driver copies this one
prepared random state before calling the fixed-grid, two-pass, and
buffered engines, so each begins with the same temperature and photon
sequence. The standalone driver defaults to this stationary procedure;
an explicit positive \verb|T_init| instead requests a transient
experiment and skips preparation. A zero \verb|T_init|, an unknown
engine name, or an unreadable or malformed namelist terminates with an
error.

\subsection{Energy bookkeeping}\label{sec:energy}

For each MC run we accumulate the event absorption
$E_{\rm abs}^{\rm evt}=\sum_{\rm events} hc/\lambda$, the continuous
absorption $4\pi a^2 H_{\rm cont}t$, and the emitted energy
$E_{\rm emit} = \int 4\pi a^2 \QplT \sigmaSB T^4\,dt$ over the
recorded trajectory only. The engines also report the change of
grain enthalpy over the recorded trajectory, $\Delta E_{\rm grain}$.
The signed closure fraction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closure}
\epsilon_E = \frac{E_{\rm abs}^{\rm evt}+4\pi a^2H_{\rm cont}t
-E_{\rm emit}-\Delta E_{\rm grain}}{E_{\rm abs}^{\rm evt}+4\pi a^2H_{\rm cont}t}
\end{equation}
separates stored energy from numerical error. It vanishes for an exact
integration of Eq.~(\ref{eq:cooling}).

\paragraph{Size-distribution runs.} In the HD23 astrodust+PAH runs at
$U=1.585$ (Section~\ref{sec:sed}), every one of the 1,946 simulated
grains of the two branch builds (default, all-MC, and
$5\times10^5$-event runs) has zero omitted and zero outside-grid time,
and $\epsilon_E$ lies between $-1.084\%$ and $+0.076\%$. The stored
term is small: $|\Delta E_{\rm grain}|$ is at most $1.2\times10^{-4}$
of the absorbed energy. The most negative values belong to the
stochastic grains: $-0.73\%$ (astrodust, $a=18.8\,\textrm{\AA}$),
$-0.79\%$ (neutral PAH, $3.5\,\textrm{\AA}$), and $-1.08\%$
(cation PAH, $13.3\,\textrm{\AA}$); the equilibrium-gate grains run
in all-MC mode reach $+0.08\%$. The two smallest astrodust grains
($a=3.548$ and $3.758\,\textrm{\AA}$) have $\epsilon_E=-0.700\%$ and
$-0.667\%$. The same grains give the same $\epsilon_E$ to within
$3.2\times10^{-6}$ with $5\times10^5$ events as with $5\times10^4$, so
the residual is not sampling noise; it is consistent with the
linearization error of the 5\% cooling step (Eq.~\ref{eq:stepcap}).
Negative $\epsilon_E$ means the trajectory emits more than it absorbs.

\paragraph{Graphite runs.} The 100\,\AA{} graphite grain at $U=1$
with $5\times10^5$ recorded events (Figure~\ref{fig:fig246}) gives
$E_{\rm emit}/(E_{\rm abs}^{\rm evt}+4\pi a^2H_{\rm cont}t)=1.0040$ and
$\epsilon_E=-0.40\%$; across the five sizes of that figure
$\epsilon_E$ ranges from $-0.29\%$ (200\,\AA) to $-0.54\%$ (10\,\AA).
The ten panels of Figure~\ref{fig:fig245} are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:fig245}.

\paragraph{Spectral normalization.} The SED builder normalizes each
stochastic grain spectrum to its absorbed power, including continuous
heating, after histogram quadrature (Eq.~\ref{eq:ec_rescale}). This
normalization enforces the stationary energy balance of the emitted
spectrum; it does not validate the sampled $P(T)$, and the closure
fractions above are measured before it.

\section{SED builder over a grain size distribution}\label{sec:sed}

The \verb|mc_sed| module integrates $P(T)$ across the HD23 astrodust+PAH
size distribution (evaluated by \verb|sed_astrodust_mod::sed_init| from
the HD23 analytic forms in \verb|size_dist.f90|) and produces the emission
SED $\lambda I_\lambda / N_{\rm H}$ in the same units as the production
\verb|sed_solve| routine. The size grid has 167 radii from
$3.548\,\textrm{\AA}$ to $5.012\umc$ for each population.

\subsection{SED accumulation}

For each grain $a_j$ in the size distribution:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sed_sum}
J_\lambda \;+\!\!= \; (dn/N_{\rm H})_j \cdot \Cabs(\lambda, a_j) \cdot
   \sum_{i} \frac{w_i}{t_{\rm total}} \cdot \pi\,\Blam(T_{{\rm mid},i}),
\end{equation}
where $w_i$ is the time spent in histogram bin $i$ (with center
$T_{{\rm mid},i}$) accumulated by the MC.  Summing over both grain
populations (astrodust + PAH) and applying the same final unit
conversion as the production solver,
$\lambda I_\lambda = \lambda J_\lambda \times 10^{-3}$, gives the
final SED in erg s$^{-1}$ sr$^{-1}$ H$^{-1}$.

\subsection{Energy normalization and charge-resolved PAH}
\label{sec:charge_rescale}

\paragraph{Single-grain energy normalization.}
Equation~(\ref{eq:sed_sum}) evaluates the Planck function at each
histogram-bin midpoint. This quadrature introduces a spectral error
whose sign depends on wavelength and the temperature distribution;
convexity does not establish a universal overestimate. The code
normalizes each stochastic grain spectrum to its absorbed power using
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ec_rescale}
s_{\rm ec} \;=\; \frac{P_{\rm abs}}{P_{\rm emit}^{\rm gray}}, \qquad
P_{\rm emit}^{\rm gray} = \sum_i w_i\, 4\pi a^2 \QplT[T_{{\rm mid},i}]
   \sigmaSB T_{{\rm mid},i}^4, \qquad
P_{\rm abs} = \frac{E_{\rm abs}^{\rm evt}}{t_{\rm total}}
   + 4\pi a^2 H_{\rm cont},
\end{equation}
where $P_{\rm abs}$ is the grain's total absorbed power (discrete
photon events plus the continuous long-wavelength heating
$H_{\rm cont}$) and $P_{\rm emit}^{\rm gray}$ is the gray bolometric
emission of the same histogram (with $w_i$ normalized to unit sum).
The normalized single-grain spectrum $s_{\rm ec}\,J_\lambda^{(j)}$
emits exactly the absorbed power while keeping the spectral shape set
by $P(T)$. For the two smallest astrodust grains, the histogram's gray
emission before normalization is $1.0070$ and $1.0066$ times the
absorbed power. In the default run the integrated MC PAH emission is
$0.9989$ times that of the production solver over the full wavelength
grid.

\paragraph{Charge-resolved PAH populations.}
The production solver treats PAHs as two charge states, neutral and
cation, mixed by an ionization fraction $f_{\rm ion}(a)$;
the two have markedly different cross sections in the near-IR (the
cation carries a \citet{Mattioda2005} NIR continuum that the neutral
lacks).  The matrix solver loops over both charge states --- each with
its own $\Cabs$, Planck-mean cooling opacity, and size-distribution
weight $dn$ --- and sums the emission. A grain of one charge state
absorbs and emits with that state's cross section; a single blended
cross section would describe a grain that does not exist. The MC
builder does the same as the matrix solver:
\verb|mc_sed_solve_pah| runs the engine twice per size, once with the
neutral $(\Cabs^{\rm neu}, dn^{\rm neu})$ and once with the cation
$(\Cabs^{\rm ion}, dn^{\rm ion})$ at an RNG seed offset by 5000, and
sums. In the default run the MC PAH emission in 1--\SI{5}{\micro\meter}
is $0.961$ times the production result
(Section~\ref{sec:compare}).

\subsection{OpenMP parallelism}

The grain loop in \verb|mc_sed::mc_sed_loop| uses an OpenMP
parallel region with a dynamically scheduled size loop. Each thread
holds private \verb|mc_grain_t| and \verb|rng_t| objects and
accumulates a local \verb|Jout_local(:)|. The spectral arrays are
combined in a critical section; scalar diagnostics use atomic updates.
With more than one thread the result is not bit-reproducible, because
the order of the floating-point sums depends on thread timing.

Each population loop calls the engine once per grain that fails the
equilibrium gate. In the default mode this is 78 of the 167 astrodust
radii and 79 of the 167 radii of each PAH charge state (236 MC
trajectories, with 265 grains on the \texttt{calc\_Teq} path); in the
all-MC mode it is all 501. With 16 OpenMP threads on the machine
described above, the current calculations took 158--198~s in default
mode, 154--213~s in all-MC mode, and 1847--2046~s for the
$5\times10^5$-event default mode. These wall times include concurrent
runs and are not a scaling measurement.

In the all-MC mode, \verb|mc_sed_loop| writes the radiation-equivalent

grain that passes the equilibrium gate, together with its $\Teq$, to

\subsection{When to use each mode}

\begin{description}
\item[Default (\texttt{F}).] The SED builder applies the equilibrium
gate and uses \texttt{calc\_Teq} for grains that pass it. Smaller
grains use the selected MC engine. This is the default calculation and
avoids an MC trajectory for 265 of the 501 grains.
\item[All-MC (\texttt{T}).] Every grain uses MC, including grains
normally treated at $\Teq$. This mode checks the equilibrium gate and
the cooling treatment; it gives the band ratios of the default mode to
$0.0003$.
\end{description}

\subsection{Step budget}

The engines integrate each waiting interval in chunks of 300 steps
(adaptive engines) within a budget of $10^6$ steps per interval. In
the default, all-MC, and $5\times10^5$-event SED runs, none of the
$2.36\times10^7$, $5.01\times10^7$, and $2.36\times10^8$ cooling
segments (both passes counted) needed a second chunk, and no interval
was left incomplete.

  \texttt{SEDust\_JKAS/results/20261031\_mc\_photon\_support/report\_extra}.}
\label{fig:pT_pah}
\end{figure}

For equilibrium grains the 250-bin grid is too coarse for the narrow
physical $P(T)$: adjacent nodes differ by a factor $1.033$. Applied
directly, \verb|calc_P| spreads the $P(T)$ of the grains from
$501\,\textrm{\AA}$ to $5\umc$ over 14--15 nodes holding more than
$10^{-3}$ each, about $\pm20\%$ around $\Teq$
(Figure~\ref{fig:pT_large}), while the MC trajectories have standard
deviations of $0.030$~K ($501\,\textrm{\AA}$), $0.007$~K
($1000\,\textrm{\AA}$), and below $0.001$~K for $a\ge0.3\umc$.
\verb|sed_solve| resolves such grains with \verb|calc_Teq| or by
narrowing the window (\verb|narrow_iterative| and the look-ahead
narrowing in \verb|sed_astrodust.f90|), and the default MC mode uses
\verb|calc_Teq| for them. In an all-MC run every simulated grain
begins preparation at its radiative-equilibrium temperature,
regardless of size. The adaptive MC engines resolve a narrow $P(T)$
when the trajectory spans more than a few bin widths; see
Section~\ref{sec:adaptive}.

\section{Adaptive-grid MC engines}\label{sec:adaptive}

The engine \verb|mc_run_engine| uses a fixed logarithmic histogram on
$[T_{\rm min}, T_{\rm max}] = [0.5, 5000]\,$K with $N_{\rm hist}$ bins
set by the caller: 600 in \verb|main_mc.x| and \verb|main_pT_compare.x|
(adjacent edges differ by a factor $1.01547$) and 2000 in the SED
builder (factor $1.00462$). This grid suits the broad distributions of
stochastically heated grains, but a narrow equilibrium distribution
falls into few of its bins. In the large-grain comparison of
Figure~\ref{fig:pT_large} (600 bins), the fixed grid places more than
$10^{-3}$ of the time in 2 bins at $501\,\textrm{\AA}$ and in a single
bin for every grain from $1000\,\textrm{\AA}$ to $5\umc$; the mean
temperature of such a histogram is the bin midpoint, up to $0.7\%$ from
$\Teq$ ($5\umc$).

To address this, two adaptive-grid engines build the histogram grid
from the observed trajectory itself.

\paragraph{Method A: two-pass with deterministic RNG replay
(\texttt{mc\_run\_engine\_2pass}).}
Pass~1 runs the full selected photon sequence without binning and
records the minimum and maximum of all temperature sub-samples. The
random state is then restored; Pass~2 repeats the same photon sequence,
integrates each waiting interval completely, and bins every sub-sample
on a grid with a margin around the measured range. Because the
integrator and the photon sampler are deterministic in their inputs,
Pass~2 traces the same trajectory as Pass~1, at about twice the cost of
a single pass: the two-pass engine took $1.4$--$2.1$ times the
fixed-grid wall time in the concurrently run comparisons of
Section~\ref{sec:per_grain_pT}. By default, $N_{\rm pass1}=\evt$. A
shorter first pass is allowed, but any Pass~2 sample outside its grid
returns an explicit range-error status; it is not silently omitted.
The engine reports its complete sub-sample count for a buffered
calculation with the same grain and random seed.

\paragraph{Method B: single-pass with sample buffer
(\texttt{mc\_run\_engine\_buffered}).}
The trajectory is integrated once, retaining each completed cooling
step's eight $(T_{\rm sub},dt_{\rm sub})$ pairs. Its full observed
range determines the adaptive histogram grid after the run. If the
caller-supplied capacity cannot hold the next complete step, the
engine returns a sample-capacity status without accepting a truncated
prefix as a complete $P(T)$. The single-grain comparison driver and the
standalone driver use the measured sub-sample count of an
identical-state two-pass run to set this capacity (for example
$5.2\times10^8$ samples, about 8~GB, for the $5\,\textrm{\AA}$
astrodust grain with $5\times10^5$ events). For the OpenMP SED builder,
capacity measurement and buffered execution are serialized so only one
grain can hold the large buffer at a time. All drivers reject
capacities above $10^9$ samples (approximately 16~GB for two real
arrays) and a count mismatch. The default SED engine is the two-pass
method.

\paragraph{Adaptive grid spacing.}
Both methods call \verb|build_adaptive_grid| with margin $\epsilon=0.05$:
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm lo} = \max\!\left[0.5\,{\rm K},\,(1-\epsilon)\,T_{\rm min}^{\rm obs}\right], \quad
T_{\rm hi} = (1+\epsilon)\,T_{\rm max}^{\rm obs}.
\end{equation}
If $T_{\rm hi}/T_{\rm lo} > 3$, the bins are log-spaced from $T_{\rm lo}$
to $T_{\rm hi}$; otherwise they are linearly spaced. The threshold
separates multi-decade stochastic excursions (where log spacing
resolves the high-$T$ tail) from narrow equilibrium distributions
(where linear spacing gives bins of uniform $\Delta T$). For the grains
of Fig.~\ref{fig:pT_large} ($2\times10^5$ events, 600 bins) the
observed ranges are $[8.78, 37.29]$~K at $100\,\textrm{\AA}$ (log
grid; $T_{\rm hi}/T_{\rm lo}=4.69$ with the margins),
$[18.244, 18.703]$~K at $501\,\textrm{\AA}$, and $[17.551, 17.645]$~K
at $1000\,\textrm{\AA}$ (linear grids); for $a\ge0.3\umc$ the observed
range is below $10^{-4}$ of $T$. Because the margin fixes the grid
width at no less than $10\%$ of $T$, a linear grid of 600 bins has bins
no narrower than $1.7\times10^{-4}\,T$. The two-pass histogram then
places more than $10^{-3}$ of the time in 30 bins at
$501\,\textrm{\AA}$, 13 bins at $1000\,\textrm{\AA}$, and 1--2 bins for
$a\ge0.3\umc$, where the distribution is narrower than one bin. Its
mean temperature agrees with $\Teq$ to $0.004\%$ for every grain from
$501\,\textrm{\AA}$ to $5\umc$. The bin lookup is $O(1)$ in both cases.

\paragraph{Runtime selection.}
\verb|main_mc_sed.x| exposes a namelist key \verb|mc_engine|
$\in\{$\texttt{'fixed'}, \texttt{'2pass'}, \texttt{'buffered'}$\}$
that dispatches grain by grain inside the OpenMP loop. The
default is \texttt{'2pass'} (adaptive). When
\verb|use_mc_for_large_grain=.true.| and \texttt{'fixed'} are
requested together, the driver promotes the selection to
\texttt{'2pass'} and logs a warning, because the fixed grid leaves
the equilibrium grains in one or two bins. A buffered calculation must
hold all required sub-samples to return a complete histogram.

\paragraph{Pass~1 vs Pass~2 length in \texttt{mc\_run\_engine\_2pass}.}
The first pass measures the range of the selected trajectory and
normally covers all $\evt$ events. A shorter probe is accepted
only if every replay sample still falls on its adaptive grid; otherwise
the engine returns a histogram-range error.

\subsection{Adaptive DA85 cutoff $\lambda_c(a)$}\label{sec:adaptive_lamc}

The \citet{DraineAnderson1985} stochastic/continuous split
(\S\ref{sec:algo}) assigns photons below $\lambda_c$ to discrete
absorption events and longer-wavelength absorption to $H_{\rm cont}$.
A fixed cutoff is not appropriate for every grain size. When a single
absorbed photon changes the temperature by much less than $\Teq$, the
grain responds to the mean heating rate, and resolving each photon is
both unnecessary and expensive. Conversely, assigning such weak
photons to rare discrete events lets the temperature relax toward the
equilibrium of the remaining continuous heating between events. The
SED builder and the comparison driver therefore use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lamc_adapt}
\boxed{\lambda_c(a) \;=\; \frac{hc}{\delta T_{\rm thresh}\,\Teq(a)\,\Ctot\!\bigl(\Teq(a),\,a\bigr)},}
\end{equation}
with $\delta T_{\rm thresh} = 0.01$. Photons with $\lambda$ short
enough that their single-event jump at $\Teq$ exceeds
$\delta T_{\rm thresh}\,\Teq$ are kept stochastic; everything longer is
folded into $H_{\rm cont}$. $\Teq(a)$ is supplied by \texttt{calc\_Teq}
on the same single-grain cross sections; $\Ctot$ is the grain heat
capacity at $\Teq$. $\lambda_c$ is clamped to the second and
second-to-last points of the wavelength grid, $0.09226$ and
$3.936\times10^4\umc$, so the split integrals have at least one bin on
each side. This is a numerical division of the absorbed spectrum, not
a physical boundary: both parts are absorbed by the grain.

At fixed temperature and material, a heat capacity proportional to
$a^3$ makes $\lambda_c$ decrease as $a^{-3}$; the actual cutoff also
depends on $\Teq(a)$ and the composition. For astrodust in the Mathis
field at $U=1.585$ the measured cutoff is $3.936\times10^4\umc$ (the
long-wavelength clamp) at $5\,\textrm{\AA}$, $7491\umc$ at
$10\,\textrm{\AA}$, $933\umc$ at $20\,\textrm{\AA}$, $57.9\umc$ at
$50\,\textrm{\AA}$, $7.08\umc$ at $100\,\textrm{\AA}$, and the
short-wavelength clamp $0.0923\umc$ for every grain from
$501\,\textrm{\AA}$ to $5\umc$. For those large grains essentially all
absorption is continuous heating, and the trajectory relaxes to $\Teq$
(Section~\ref{sec:compare}).

For the two smallest astrodust grains of the size grid
($a=3.548$ and $3.758\,\textrm{\AA}$), $\lambda_c$ reaches the
long-wavelength clamp. Their continuous heating is
$H_{\rm cont}=5.4944\times10^{-19}$ and
$5.8200\times10^{-19}\,{\rm erg\,cm^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$, which is
$4.3\times10^{-14}$ of their event power. The root of
$\QplT\sigma T^4=H_{\rm cont}$ with the implemented
$\langle\Qabs\rangle_{1\,\rm K}$ is $0.02976$~K for both, below both
the $0.5$~K floor and the 1~K lower bound of the $\QplT$ table; it is
not a validated physical dust temperature. In the default SED run
these grains spend $0.546$ and $0.473$ of the recorded time at the
floor, and their two-pass histograms hold $0.592$ and $0.521$ of the
time in bins below $0.55$~K. Repeating the two calculations with the
same seeds and preparation (a separate driver that calls the same
engine routines and reproduces the logged event power, elapsed time,
and floor time of the SED run exactly), the gray emission of those
cold bins is $2.1\times10^{-9}$ and $1.8\times10^{-9}$ of the absorbed
power. The floor therefore does not change these grains' emitted
power or spectrum at any measurable level, but the cold part of their
$P(T)$ is a numerical construct of the floor and of the 1~K table
limit, not a prediction. The same grains reach $5140$~K and $4180$~K
after their hottest photon absorptions; the fixed-grid engine, whose
grid ends at 5000~K, therefore refuses the first of them with a
histogram-range status, while the adaptive engines accept both.
