G18 Model D dust model (DustEM distribution)
============================================

Source: DustEM, https://www.ias.u-psud.fr/DUSTEM/
        Compiegne et al. (2011), A&A 525, A103 -- the code
        Guillet et al. (2018), A&A 610, A16    -- the model (their Model D)

Files are taken verbatim from the DustEM distribution and are NOT modified,
with one exception -- the two G_ files, which the distribution does not have
and which SEDust computes; they are described further down:

  GRAIN_G17_ModelD.DAT     DustEM data/    -- model definition (3 populations)
  ALIGN_G17_ModelD.DAT     DustEM data/    -- alignment law (polarization)
  oprop/LAMBDA.DAT         DustEM oprop/   -- the 800-point wavelength grid
  oprop/Q_<type>.DAT       DustEM oprop/   -- Qabs, Qsca per radius, averaged
                                              over orientation
  oprop/Q1_<type>.DAT      DustEM oprop/   -- Qabs, Qsca for E parallel to the
                                              projected magnetic field
  oprop/Q2_<type>.DAT      DustEM oprop/   -- ... and for E perpendicular to it
  oprop/G_PAH0_MC10.DAT    DustEM oprop/   -- g = <cos theta>, PAH only.  Every
                                              value in it is 0: DustEM ships
                                              the file but not a computed g for
                                              this PAH population.
  hcap/C_<type>.DAT        DustEM hcap/    -- heat capacity, erg K^-1 cm^-3

Q1_ and Q2_ exist for the two populations whose GRAIN line carries the `pol`
keyword, amCBE_0.3333x and aSil2001BE6pctG_0.4x, and for those two only.  They
have the layout of Q_ -- nsize, the radii, a QABS block and a QSCA block -- and
are what the polarized cross sections of this model are formed from,
Cpol = pi a^2 (Q2_abs - Q1_abs)/2 and its extinction twin.  PAH0_MC10 carries
no `pol` keyword and has neither file.

SCATTERING ASYMMETRY: COMPUTED HERE, NOT DISTRIBUTED
====================================================

The DustEM distribution ships no G_amCBE_0.3333x.DAT and no
G_aSil2001BE6pctG_0.4x.DAT.  Guillet et al. (2018) computed no asymmetry
parameter -- the paper contains no such quantity -- and DustEM reads a G_ file
only under its `pdr` run keyword, which this model does not set, so its own
EXT_G17_ModelD run needs none.  Since the two populations carry 90% of the
dust mass, the model as distributed has no <cos theta> at all, and a
radiative-transfer host handed g = 0 for a model whose albedo reaches 0.38
would scatter isotropically and never know.

SEDust therefore computes those two tables:

  oprop/G_amCBE_0.3333x.DAT         computed by SEDust, NOT from DustEM
  oprop/G_aSil2001BE6pctG_0.4x.DAT  computed by SEDust, NOT from DustEM

with tmatrix/driver/spheroid_asymmetry_table.f90, from the same materials,
shapes, size grids and wavelength grid the distributed Q_ tables are built on.
Each file's own header records the method, the census of which regime produced
each entry, and the measured bound on the one approximation it contains; the
short version is:

  material   amCBE_0.3333x         BE amorphous carbon of Zubko et al. (1996),
                                   data/dielectric/index_amcBE_ZMCB96
             aSil2001BE6pctG_0.4x  the WD01 "smoothed UV" astrosilicate,
                                   data/dielectric/eps_suvSil, with 6% by
                                   volume of the same BE a-C as Maxwell
                                   Garnett inclusions (Guillet et al. Eq. 28)
  shape      prolate spheroids, axis ratio b/a = 1/3 and 0.4 (their Table 3,
             row D), random orientation, with Q and g defined against the
             volume-equivalent radius a_V (their Sect. 3.6)
  method     x = 2 pi a_V/lambda < 0.1  : g = 0, the exact Rayleigh dipole
                                          value, correct to O(x^2)
             0.1 <= x <= 50, converged : Mishchenko's random-orientation
                                          T-matrix, which is the analytic form
                                          of the 768-direction HEALPix
                                          orientation average of their Eq. 19
             otherwise                 : g of the volume-equivalent sphere by
                                          Mie theory at the same m -- an
                                          approximation, and the same class of
                                          stand-in Guillet et al. used in the
                                          ultraviolet (their Eqs. 11-14, after
                                          Min et al. 2003)

The two refractive-index tables are copies of Grain/opt/dat/index_amcBE_ZMCB96
and Grain/opt/eps_suvSil, put under data/dielectric/ so that these G files can
be regenerated from the release alone.  Regenerate with

  cd tmatrix && make spheroid_asymmetry_table.x && ./spheroid_asymmetry_table.x

which rewrites both files and, in tmatrix/output/, the diagnostics that record
regime and tolerance for every cell.

HOW THEY WERE VALIDATED.  Four checks, all with figures under docs/figs/ and
numbers in docs/SEDust_user_manual.tex:

  1  this tree's Mie with index_amcBE_ZMCB96 against DustEM's own BE a-C
     SPHERE tables oprop/Q_amCBEx.DAT and G_amCBEx.DAT
  2  this tree's Mie with eps_suvSil against Draine's own sphere table
     opt/suvSil_81
  3  the random-orientation T-matrix Q_abs and Q_sca of the two spheroid
     populations against the distributed Q_ tables, over the cells where the
     solver converged.  This is the check that tests the Maxwell Garnett rule,
     the two axis ratios, the a_V convention and our orientation average
     against their HEALPix average at once, and it is what makes the g from
     the same evaluation trustworthy.
  4  the T-matrix g against the volume-equivalent sphere's g across the
     converged region, which is what bounds the stand-in of regime 3.

  Checks 1 and 2 are run by tmatrix/compare_mie_spheres.x; checks 3 and 4 are
  reported by spheroid_asymmetry_table.x itself.

The two large populations are PROLATE spheroids (the 0.3333x and 0.4x in their
names are the axis ratios).  Their Q_<type>.DAT tables are already averaged
over orientation, so they enter the SCALAR model exactly as a sphere table
does.  The POLARIZED model reads Q1_/Q2_<type>.DAT and ALIGN_G17_ModelD.DAT in
addition, and the ALIGN keyword line carries no ANIS, so the unpolarized
extinction stays the plain orientation average with no alignment correction --
which is what DustEM computes for this model.  The alignment law that file
states is the parametric one of Guillet et al. (2018),

  f_align(a) = 0.5 f_max [1 + tanh( ln(a/a_align) / p_stiff )]

with a_align = 8.8647328317E-02 um, p_stiff = 2.7000692487E-01 and
f_max = 6.6593271494E-01, applied to every `pol` population (the keyword line
says `univ`).  It is applied as a size weight inside the size integral, not
folded into the cross sections, which is where DustEM applies it too.

HD23 (Hensley & Draine 2023, ApJ 948, 55, Sec. 6.2.2 and Fig. 16) compare
against this model, running DustEM on GRAIN_G17_ModelD.DAT and adopting a
dust-to-gas mass ratio 0.0081 (Guillet et al. 2018, Table 3).  The three
populations of GRAIN_G17_ModelD.DAT themselves sum to Mdust/MH = 7.271e-3.

NOT from DustEM, generated here:

  kext_g18d[_euv].dat       sed/calc_kext.x -- the size-integrated extinction
  sedust_g18d.h5            sed/calc_qtable.x -- /grid (LAMBDA.DAT) and
                            /qtable/<population> (the Q_/G_ tables above, put
                            on each population's own model radii), plus /kext
                            from sed/calc_kext.x.  All three populations carry
                            a g dataset, so the model reaches a host with a
                            real <cos theta>.  The product holds SCALAR optics
                            only: the polarized cross sections are formed at
                            build time from the Q1_/Q2_ text tables, whichever
                            source the scalar optics came from.
                            Regenerate with
                              ./calc_qtable.x g18d && ./calc_kext.x g18d euv
                            in that order: the first replaces the file.

reference/ -- DustEM's own output for GRAIN_G17_ModelD.DAT
==========================================================

The DustEM distribution ships no published result for this model, so the four
files here were produced by building DustEM 4.3 from the sources in the same
distribution and running it on these very input files, with
ALIGN_G17_ModelD.DAT copied to data/ALIGN.DAT as the model requires:

  reference/EXT_G17_ModelD.RES       extinction, DustEM out/EXT.RES
  reference/SED_G17_ModelD.RES       emission,   DustEM out/SED.RES
  reference/EXT_POL_G17_ModelD.RES   polarized extinction, DustEM out/EXT_POL.RES
  reference/SED_POL_G17_ModelD.RES   polarized emission,   DustEM out/SED_POL.RES

The two _POL_ files come from the same run: DustEM writes them whenever the
GRAIN file declares a `pol` population and ALIGN.DAT names a `lin` law, which
this model does.  EXT_POL_G17_ModelD.RES has the shape of EXT_G17_ModelD.RES --
'#' header, a "ntype nwave" line, then rows of lambda[um], ABS(1..ntype),
SCA(1..ntype) and the total, all in cm^2 per H times 1e21 -- and its ABS and
SCA columns are DustEM's size integrals of 0.5*(Q2-Q1)*f_pol for absorption and
for scattering.  sed/test_dustem_polarized_extinction.x checks this tree
against it, column by column, and reaches 5e-7 relative.  SED_POL_G17_ModelD.RES
is DustEM's polarized SED, 4*pi*nu*I_nu/NH; no driver here writes a polarized
SED, so it is kept as the record of what DustEM computes rather than as a
comparison this tree runs -- the emission side reaches through the lamI_pol
argument of dust_emission, and comparing against this file would be a
comparison of two stochastic-heating solvers.

The build was checked first on THEMIS, where the distribution does publish a
result: it reproduces the distributed EXT_J13.RES exactly and SED_J13.RES to
1.6e-5 over 1-3000 um.  Longward of 3000 um the distributed SED_J13.RES is
larger than this build gives, by a factor that grows to 230 at 2.5 cm, where
the emission is six orders of magnitude below its peak; the distributed
data/GRAIN.DAT of that release sets run keywords this model file does not
(zdistf, and charge on the nano-carbon population), which is the likely
reason.  Nothing in the 1-3000 um band used for comparison is affected.
